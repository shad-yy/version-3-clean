# Sky Sports, TNT Sports, & Prime Video: Cost to Watch Premier League 2026-27

The total cost to watch all televised Premier League 2026-27 matches in the UK is **£82.99 per month** across Sky Sports, TNT Sports, and Amazon Prime Video. No single subscription covers the full televised schedule, because the rights are split deliberately between three broadcasters.

With inflation and rights fees rising, football fans are paying more than ever to support their teams. In the UK, rights are heavily fragmented, meaning that one subscription is no longer enough to watch even half of the televised games. This article analyzes the real cost of following the 2026-27 season and outlines how you can save hundreds of pounds.

## Official Subscription Costs Breakdown (UK)

To watch every official broadcast of the 2026-27 campaign, a UK household must maintain active subscriptions with three separate companies.

### 1. Sky Sports — The Lion's Share
Sky Sports hosts the largest package, showing 128 live matches.
*   **Monthly Rate:** Starts at £43/month.
*   **Contract Term:** Typically 18 months, with steep price hikes after the promotional period.
*   **Total Season Cost:** ~£516.

### 2. TNT Sports — The Saturday Lunchtime Fixture
TNT Sports holds the rights to 52 matches, primarily the Saturday 12:30 PM slot.
*   **Monthly Rate:** £30.99 via the discovery+ Premium app.
*   **Contract Term:** Month-to-month.
*   **Total Season Cost:** ~£309.90.

### 3. Amazon Prime Video — The Festive Rounds
Amazon Prime Video streams two full rounds of fixtures in December.
*   **Monthly Rate:** £8.99/month.
*   **Contract Term:** Month-to-month (many fans subscribe only for December).
*   **Total Season Cost:** ~£17.98 (for 2 months).

**Combined Total Cost:** **£843.88 per year** for the 200 televised games.

## The Big Missing Piece: Saturday 3 PM Blackout

Even if you pay over £840 a year for all three subscriptions, you are still blocked from watching **180 matches** due to the UK's Saturday 3 PM broadcasting blackout. If your team plays at 3 PM on Saturday, you cannot watch them live on TV in the UK.

For international viewers in the US (via Peacock/NBC Sports) or Australia (via Optus Sport), this rule does not apply. They get access to all 380 games for a single, low-cost subscription.

---

## Why the UK Bill Is Higher Than Elsewhere

The gap is a rights-structure difference, not a pricing accident.

The UK operates a **Saturday 3pm blackout**: no match kicking off between 2:45pm and 5:15pm on a Saturday may be broadcast live in the UK. The rule dates to the 1960s and exists to protect attendance at lower-league grounds. It means roughly 180 of the 380 fixtures each season are never televised here at all — no UK subscription, at any price, includes them.

Markets without that rule sell a single all-inclusive package instead, which is why a US or Australian viewer pays once and sees every game.

### How to Reduce the Bill Legitimately

*   **Use NOW Sports passes seasonally.** Sky Sports content on a rolling monthly pass, cancellable over the summer break — typically three months of savings a year.
*   **Pick one broadcaster by competition.** If the Champions League matters more than full Premier League coverage, TNT Sports alone roughly halves the cost.
*   **Watch the free tier.** *Match of the Day* highlights on BBC iPlayer, plus the FA Cup and England internationals live under Ofcom "Listed Events" rules, cost nothing.

Prices quoted are the advertised UK rates at the time of writing and change regularly — confirm with the broadcaster before subscribing.
